## Why this exists

New operators are expected to be able to process and verify an accident alert after a
short training session, not a long one. This page is that session: work through it in
order, in about 15 minutes, and the first real alert you handle won't be the first time
you've seen any of this.

## 1. Get oriented (about 3 minutes)

Read [Getting Started with ADAS](getting-started-with-adas) if you haven't already. It
covers what the system does, the two account types, and where things live in the
sidebar. If the sidebar itself is still unfamiliar, [Navigating the
Sidebar](navigating-the-sidebar) walks through every section.

## 2. Set up your alert sound (about 2 minutes)

Open **Profile** and set an alarm sound and volume you'll actually notice from across
the room. See [Choosing Your Alarm Sound and Volume](choosing-your-alarm-sound-and-volume).
Do this before anything else — an alert you don't hear is a missed alert.

## 3. Know what you're watching (about 3 minutes)

Open **Cameras** and skim the list so you recognize the names and locations that will
show up in an alert. See [Camera Connection Status vs. AI Status](camera-connection-vs-ai-status)
for the difference between a camera being connected and a camera actively detecting.

## 4. Learn the two actions you'll use most (about 5 minutes)

Read [Confirming an Accident Alert](confirming-an-accident-alert) and [Dismissing a
False Positive](dismissing-a-false-positive) back to back. Together they cover every
decision the alert popup ever asks you to make: is this real, or isn't it.

## 5. Know what happens next (about 2 minutes)

Skim [The Accident Alert Popup and the Alarm](the-accident-alert-popup-and-the-alarm) so
the full-screen popup and its siren aren't a surprise the first time they appear, and
[Reviewing Ongoing Incidents in the Tray](reviewing-ongoing-incidents-in-the-tray) so you
know where a confirmed incident goes after you act on it.

## You're ready when

You can answer these without looking anything up:

- What does the Confirm button do, and when does it stop being available?
- What does Dismiss do, and is it reversible?
- Where does an incident go after you confirm it, and how do you find it again?
- Why does a camera stop detecting after it raises an alert?

If any of those feel shaky, revisit the linked article above rather than guessing during
a real alert — this Help Center stays open and searchable while you work.
